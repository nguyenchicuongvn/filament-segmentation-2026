## Ideas

### Instance-normalised loss: closed-form blob Dice plus a BiCC-style predicted-component term, on the N1 soft-target recipe (with EMA added at no cost)
- **mechanism**: PQ counts each instance once, but BCE + per-image Dice weights pixels, so large filaments set the gradient. Measured symptom: small instances come out 1.39x / 1.23x too large (F13), and 56% of FP entries are near-miss or partial overlaps with a single GT. A per-GT-instance Dice term (blob: Dice of instance i computed with all other GT instances masked out) gives each filament equal weight and directly rewards getting each instance's extent above IoU 0.5. That targets the 289 near-miss and 157 partial FP and the 217 near-miss FN. The predicted-component (BiCC) term thresholds the detached prediction, runs connected components, and adds a per-component loss (mean p over pixels outside GT) for components that do not overlap GT. That targets the 120 true look-alike FP and the merge/fragment errors, each component on its own scale. Closed form keeps it cheap: per-instance sums via scatter_add on a label map; Dice_i = 2a_i / (P_total - (F - a_i) + |y_i|), where a_i is the p-sum on instance i, F the p-sum on all GT, P_total the p-sum overall. Instances come from one randomly sampled annotator per step, as in umerkang66; the soft map stays the BCE target. EMA 0.998 is about 10 lines (torch.optim.swa_utils.AveragedModel). It costs no GPU time, and the E9a history (FP 1163-1729 between epochs 15 and 20) shows mid-run instability.
- **evidence**: Measured here: FP kinds near-miss 36%, partial 20%, merge 8%, true look-alike 15%; FN near-miss 22%. The encoder upgrade did not move near-miss (301→289) or merge (66→67), so capacity alone does not fix these buckets. Arithmetic: 50 near-miss conversions = +0.012. External: BiCC, theainerd S10b, re-read: +0.0095 mean over 5 folds, 4/5 positive, no CI. Blob bundle, umerkang66 S11a: +0.0092, 90% CI [+0.006,+0.012], R34 proxy. JML1 alone +0.0003, so a plain IoU-surrogate loss (Lovasz/Jaccard) is not the active ingredient; per-instance normalisation is (inference). arXiv 2609.30223: BiCC beats blob loss on 5/5 datasets. EMA: no isolated number anywhere (S11b); E9a ep25 vs ep30 0.3811 vs 0.3809 suggests an annealed OneCycle already averages, so expect EMA ≤ +0.003 (guess).
- **expected_gain_pq**: +0.008 (plausible range -0.003 to +0.018). That is half of the two published ~+0.009 gains plus a little for BiCC beating blob. Guess, not measured.
- **gpu_hours**: 6.5
- **dev_hours**: 12
- **cpu_testable_now**: False
- **test_plan**: Control: N1 fold 0 (effb4_s1024_f0, soft targets, same 30 epochs, same seed, same checkpoint rule). Treatment: the same plus blob (weight 1.0) + BiCC component term (weight 0.3, as theainerd) + EMA weights for validation and export. First, a Mac smoke test at 256 px, 2 epochs, 20 images: unit-test the closed-form blob Dice against a brute-force per-instance loop (tolerance 1e-5); check the loss is finite with zero-instance crops and that EMA state_dict round-trips. Metric: scripts/compare.py, paired bootstrap by image on fold 0 (~141 images), 'tune:hyst' cross-tuned grid. Accept if Δ ≥ +0.012 and CI > 0 (PLAN § 3). If 0 < Δ < 0.012, run fold 1 (+1.3 h) and accept if the pooled CI > 0. Reject if Δ ≤ 0. Also report the FP-kind breakdown (fp_kinds.py) on both arms: the mechanism is supported only if near-miss + partial FP fall by ≥ 10%. On acceptance, roll out folds 1-4 (~5.2 h).
- **risks**: Fails if near-misses come from annotator style rather than model error. Of FN, 38% are unsupported multi-annotator GT, and human-vs-human SQ is only 0.634, so many near-misses may be irreducible. BiCC as implemented by theainerd is unknown (the README gives no formula); my BiCC-lite may not reproduce it. Their gain was on 512 native crops with a B3 at 0.423 per fold, not at 1024 full-disk. The per-annotator instance sampling adds target noise. A per-instance loss can over-fragment, raising the 1:n rate, which the rubric also scores. One fold cannot see +0.008 (CI ±0.012), so the likely outcome is the 'in between, run fold 1' branch: budget 2.6 h, not 1.3 h. Needs a new cache of per-annotator label maps (uint16 at 1024), and the cache bug history (E12-E14) says to test the atomic writes.

### Do not spend GPU on small-filament recall or limb-corrected input; move that budget to extent/grouping (drop the limb part of N3, keep N2 as a test of SQ, not of small recall)
- **mechanism**: This is a negative recommendation that redirects budget, measured on CPU. F12 (54% of GT < 400 px untouched) motivated resolution and small-object work, but humans do no better: one annotator's recall of another's <400 px GT is 0.33 vs the model's 0.32. Only 37% of small GT in multi-annotator images are drawn by a second annotator. Even a perfect fix of 30 small TP that also bring 30 FP is worth +0.003. Limb input (3-plane, S10a) targets limb look-alikes, but the none-matched rate is flat with radius (0.29/0.28/0.25/0.31 up to r/R 0.9), and true look-alikes are only 15% of FP. So a limb-correction channel has a ceiling well under +0.005 on our model. Whatever N2 (2048 crops) gains should come through SQ and near-misses on mid-size filaments, where near-misses concentrate (1600-3200 px holds 28%).
- **evidence**: All measured here (fp_fn_anatomy.py, summary_effb4.txt): human vs model small recall 0.33/0.32; small GT support rate 0.37; flat none-matched rate by r/R; true look-alike FP 120/794. External, consistent with this: umerkang66's limb PQ 0.37 is for their model and does not transfer. S9e: CLAHE (an input normalisation) hurt biconcavelens.
- **expected_gain_pq**: 0 direct; it frees ~1-2 GPU h and ~4 dev h for idea 1 and avoids a likely null result
- **gpu_hours**: 0
- **dev_hours**: 0.5
- **cpu_testable_now**: True
- **test_plan**: Already measured. To confirm with N2: when effb4_c2048_f0 maps arrive, rerun fp_fn_anatomy.py and fp_kinds.py on fold 0 for both N1 and N2. Prediction: N2's Δ will come from near-miss/SQ (SQ up ≥ 0.008) rather than from <400 px recall (up ≤ 0.05). If <400 px recall rises by > 0.10 with FP flat, this recommendation is wrong and small-object work deserves budget.
- **risks**: The human-recall comparison is pooled over all train images while the model figure is on 213 val images; group mix differs slightly. Limb look-alikes could matter more on test if test sites differ (F10 says the test maps look like val, so unlikely). The 3-plane input might help through better contrast normalisation rather than fewer limb FP; that remains untested and is not ruled out.

### Mask R-CNN detector member for grouping errors (N4), only if idea 1 is decided by 2026-11-02
- **mechanism**: Instance grouping is the second-largest training-addressable bucket and the one a semantic U-Net plus connected components cannot learn. Measured: merge 67 + fragment 24 FP entries, merged 149 + split 88 FN entries, about 11% of FP and 25% of FN. The encoder upgrade did not reduce merges (66→67), and rule-based splitting/merging only trades one error for the other (F23). A detector predicts instances directly and gives a per-instance score. That score could also fix the weak confidence ranking we measured (AUC 0.72; peak 1.0 for >75% of FPs), which explains why the GBM filter and hysteresis gains are small.
- **evidence**: Measured here: grouping-error counts above; confidence AUC 0.724. External: biconcavelens S9a: Mask R-CNN 0.429 vs their U-Net 0.396, but their U-Net is weaker than ours (0.408), so the head-to-head gain should be smaller (inference). Score-weighted mask voting adds +0.007-0.012 with CI > 0 in only 3/5 splits (S9c), and isotonic calibration is mandatory (−0.021 without it, S9b). torchvision Mask R-CNN is BSD.
- **expected_gain_pq**: +0.008 for the U-Net + Mask R-CNN ensemble (range −0.005 to +0.025); alone, Mask R-CNN ≈ U-Net ± 0.015. Guess anchored on S9a/S9c.
- **gpu_hours**: 7.5
- **dev_hours**: 20
- **cpu_testable_now**: False
- **test_plan**: Control: N1 (or idea 1 if accepted) fold 0 maps. Treatment: Mask R-CNN R50-FPN at 1024 trained on fold 0 (instances from one sampled annotator per step), with masks voted into the U-Net map with isotonic-calibrated scores fitted inside fold 0 by 5-fold CV, overlaps resolved into one label image, and an assert that masks do not overlap. Metric: compare.py paired bootstrap on fold 0, cross-tuned. Accept if Δ ≥ +0.012 and CI > 0, and merge + split errors fall by ≥ 20%. Go/no-go by 2026-11-05 (G3), as in PLAN.
- **risks**: This is the biggest dev cost (about 20 h) against an implementation deadline of 31/10 that it cannot meet. Merge/split errors are partly annotator disagreement about whether broken filaments are one instance (S18, open question to the host), which the detector cannot resolve. Score calibration and voting add complexity that judges penalise (S6b). TTA for a detector is awkward. Our cc/hyst grouping already merges within 2×group_radius, so the residual gain may be small. If GPU time is short after idea 1's rollout, this is the item to cut.

### Annotator-conditioned training with condition dropout (style head), predicting the consensus at test
- **mechanism**: 411 of 707 train images have one annotator, and their hard idiosyncratic outlines are learned as if they were truth. Per-annotator matched-area ratios run 0.90-1.33 and per-annotator PQ 0.286-0.549. Feed the annotator group (01-06, plus 'unknown') as constant one-hot input planes or FiLM. Train on per-annotator targets with the group condition, and with probability 0.3 on condition 'unknown' with the soft-fraction target. At test, run 'unknown'. The model can then attribute extent style (for example group 01's) to the condition instead of to the image, which in principle sharpens the consensus extent behind the near-miss bucket.
- **evidence**: Measured (existing log critique2_errors_effb4_hyst.txt): per-group PQ 0.373-0.461; per-annotator area ratio 0.90-1.33. Measured here: near-misses are 36% of FP. No external competitor reports this; ranjeet258 (S12) models per-annotator IoU only in a post-hoc GBM, without numbers. Gain is pure inference. E8 (group channel) is a different thing (an instance-group map, +0.003).
- **expected_gain_pq**: +0.003 (range −0.005 to +0.010); a guess with no anchor
- **gpu_hours**: 1.5
- **dev_hours**: 6
- **cpu_testable_now**: False
- **test_plan**: Only run if budget remains after idea 1's rollout. Control: N1 fold 0. Treatment: the same with conditioning; at test, condition = unknown. Paired bootstrap on fold 0, cross-tuned. Accept if Δ ≥ +0.012 and CI > 0. Diagnostic: per-group PQ spread should not widen. Smoke test on Mac: check the 'unknown' output equals the mean of the group-conditioned outputs to within 0.05 on the soft target.
- **risks**: In expectation the 'unknown' head learns the same soft fraction as N1, so the gain may be exactly zero. With 37 annotators and 213-image folds, the conditioning can overfit. Test annotators are unknown (S7), so no group-specific benefit is reachable at inference. This is the weakest-evidenced idea; it is listed so the team does not spend on it before ideas 1 and 3.
