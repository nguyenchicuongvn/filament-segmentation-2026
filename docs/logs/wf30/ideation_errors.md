## Measurements
- Object-level forensics of E9a holdout + strict hysteresis: scripts/research/wf30/errors/collect.py, then analyze.py (output in analyze.out in the same folder). It reproduces the baseline exactly: PQ 0.4103, SQ 0.668, TP 1401 / FP 794 / FN 965, 337 annotator entries. 'PQ if fixed' assumes an oracle fixes only that bucket; the buckets overlap and the gains do not add.
- FP buckets (share of 794; oracle PQ gain if all removed): (1) the prediction matches ANOTHER annotator's GT on the same image: 228 (28.7%), +0.022. It cannot be removed without losing that other annotator's TP. (2) Orphan on the disk interior: 169 (21.3%), +0.016. (3) Fragment lying mostly inside one GT with IoU < 0.3: 148 (18.6%), +0.014. Only 14% of the GTs containing these fragments are matched by another prediction, and 38% of them are 'split' FNs. (4) Near-miss too thin (IoU 0.3-0.5): 143, +0.013. (5) Near-miss too fat: 52, +0.005. (6) Merge touching 2 or more GTs: 43, +0.004. (7) Location buckets are almost empty: orphans beyond ±70° longitude 0, limb r > 0.92 1, near a sunspot proxy 2, together +0.0003. Removing every FP gives 0.497.
- Location check (S14): the model already produces almost nothing beyond ±70° (1 of 2194 predictions). GTs there: 0.1%. Precision by longitude: 0.63 (0-30°), 0.67 (30-50°), 0.59 (50-60°), 0.64 (60-70°). Recall drops at 60-70° (0.20) and at r 0.9-0.95 (0.17), but only 50 and 30 GTs are there. Near a sunspot (distance < 0.05 R) precision is 0.63, the same as elsewhere. So the limb, ±70° and sunspot hypotheses cover under 1% of errors.
- FN buckets (share of 965): missed with no touching prediction on a single-annotator image: 214 (22.2%, median 608 px at 2048). Missed and drawn by only this annotator on a multi-annotator image ('solo'): 190 (19.7%). Missed although another annotator also drew it: only 63 (6.5%), ceiling +0.011 if all became TP at IoU 0.62. Near-miss too thin: 140 (median GT 1816 px, predicted/GT area median 0.58, quartiles 0.47-0.69). Near-miss too fat: 94 (median 772 px, ratio 1.97). Merged into a neighbour's prediction: 137. Split where the union of fragments would reach IoU > 0.5: 58 (median 4255 px). Partial: 66.
- Counterfactual fixes (whole bucket): all near-misses to TP at 0.55 gives +0.056 (thin +0.034, fat +0.023). Splits merged gives +0.021. Merges split by proportional partition gives +0.028. Raising every TP IoU to at least 0.70 gives +0.033, at least 0.75 gives +0.055.
- Annotator disagreement drives the near-miss bucket: in multi-annotator images, 59% of near-miss predictions are TP for another annotator (36% overall). Prediction-level agreement: on 2-annotator images, of 331 predictions 91 match nobody, 73 match one annotator and 167 match both. On 3-annotator images, of 242 predictions 40/50/55/97 match 0/1/2/3 annotators. Small filaments on multi-annotator images: GTs under 400 px are drawn by only one annotator 63% of the time; recall is 0.17 for those and 0.50 when another annotator also drew them.
- By annotator group (PQ, top FP and FN buckets): 01 PQ 0.384, FP/TP 0.69, 40% of FPs are other-annotator, top FN is solo misses 32%. 04 PQ 0.373, near-thin is 30% of FPs, splits 12% of FNs (draws larger single masks). 03 PQ 0.455 and 06 PQ 0.443: 68% and 55% of FNs are misses on single-annotator images (they draw many small filaments). 05: 47% of FPs are other-annotator, 25% of FNs are merges.
- Orphan FP vs TP prediction separability, single-feature AUC: mean probability 0.852, area 0.801, elongation 0.683, contrast 0.41, darkness 0.40, radius 0.48, sunspot distance 0.51. Location and darkness carry no signal; confidence and size are already used by the GBM filter.
- Component tree (H6 tested directly): tree_collect.py builds 16,156 nested candidates from threshold levels 0.1-0.95 with group radius 4, IoU against every annotator at 2048. With a fixed instance set the PQ denominator is constant, so a tree DP gives the exact best selection. Baseline rebuilt from tree nodes: 0.4119 (real pipeline 0.4103, difference +0.002 [-0.001, +0.005]). ORACLE node selection (Dinkelbach): PQ 0.5352 (+0.125). Taking every node at one fixed level: 0.394 / 0.396 / 0.397 at 0.4 / 0.5 / 0.6.
- Learned tree selection: tree_select_fast.py, a GBM on 20 node features (level, probability statistics, shape, contrast, position, parent/child area ratios, depth). 5-fold out-of-fold by image, lambda grid. Results: 0.3978 / 0.4045 / 0.4068 / 0.4032 at lambda 0.35 / 0.45 / 0.55 / 0.65. With lambda cross-tuned, PQ 0.4059 vs baseline -0.0043 [-0.0110, +0.0026] (paired bootstrap over 213 images). So the +0.125 oracle headroom is not learnable from map and image features; per-instance post-processing on these maps is exhausted, which extends F21 and argues against H6.

## Ideas

### Stop post-processing work and drop location/sunspot priors; spend the remaining budget only on model-side changes (a re-prioritisation backed by measurement)
- **mechanism**: Removes work that cannot pay off. Two measurements show it. First, location, limb and sunspot errors are under 1% of all errors (oracle ceiling +0.0003), so the ±70° limb-corrected input in N3 cannot be justified by FP reduction. Second, a learned choice of which component-tree nodes to keep (threshold level, dropping, fragment choice) is -0.004 [-0.011, +0.003] out-of-fold, against an oracle of +0.125. The headroom exists, but these features cannot see it: it is mostly which annotator drew what. Any further post-processing variant should be presumed null unless it adds a new input signal.
- **evidence**: Measured: location buckets 3/794 FPs; 1/2194 predictions beyond 70°; precision flat with longitude and sunspot distance (0.59-0.67). Tree oracle 0.535, learned 0.406 vs base 0.410. Location/darkness/contrast AUC 0.40-0.51. Consistent with the ledger: R4, R4b, R4c and R5a/b all gave +0.000 to +0.007, non-additive (F21). External: S14 says annotators skip beyond ±70°; the model already learned this.
- **expected_gain_pq**: 0 directly; saves about 2-4 dev hours and keeps N3 honest (EMA and blob loss must earn their place without the limb argument)
- **gpu_hours**: 0
- **dev_hours**: 0.5
- **cpu_testable_now**: True
- **test_plan**: Already measured; the scripts are in the scratchpad. Record in EXPERIMENTS: tree oracle 0.535, learned -0.004 [-0.011, +0.003], location buckets under 1%. For N3, run the limb-corrected input as its own ablation only if the bundle passes: drop it unless it alone gives at least +0.010 with CI > 0.
- **risks**: The learned selector used 20 hand-made features and one GBM. A richer learner (CNN on candidate crops) could do better, but it would be a new model rather than post-processing. Sunspot detection was a crude proxy (flattened intensity < 0.55, compact); 15% of images had no detected spot. A different model (N1/N2) could put FPs in other places, so re-run analyze.py on new out-of-fold maps.

### Gate N2 (2048 crops) hard: the small-filament recall it targets is mostly annotator-idiosyncratic
- **mechanism**: N2's premise is that missed small filaments are a resolution problem (F12). The breakdown shows most misses are filaments no other annotator drew: 214 on single-annotator images, 190 'solo' on multi-annotator images, and only 63 also drawn by another annotator. Higher resolution can only recover the consensus ones reliably; the solo ones are annotator style. Ceiling for recovering every co-drawn miss: +0.011. Plus SQ on small TPs: TP IoU is 0.623 under 400 px vs the 0.735→0.881 resolution ceiling from F14.
- **evidence**: Measured: 63 co-drawn misses give +0.011 at IoU 0.62. GTs under 400 px on multi-annotator images are 63% single-annotator; recall 0.17 for those, 0.50 for co-drawn ones. Small predictions are too fat: predicted/GT area 1.39 under 400 px. F14 resolution ceiling: under 800 px, 0.860 at 1024 vs 0.881 at 1536 (small). S13 found -0.07 at higher resolution in another team (cited in RESEARCH).
- **expected_gain_pq**: +0.003 to +0.010 at most from N2; probably below the detection limit of one fold (±0.012)
- **gpu_hours**: 0
- **dev_hours**: 0.5
- **cpu_testable_now**: True
- **test_plan**: When N2 fold 0 arrives, run collect.py/analyze.py on its maps vs N1 fold 0 on the same images. N2 must cut the co-drawn and near-fat FN buckets by at least 30% and give ΔPQ ≥ +0.012 with CI > 0 (the existing gate). If ΔPQ is under +0.012, do not spend more GPU on 2048 folds.
- **risks**: On test, the group mix may favour annotators who draw small filaments (03/06: 55-68% of their FNs are single-annotator misses). Higher recall on small filaments could then help the LB more than val shows; that is unverifiable (H5). Do not choose by LB.

### Instance-partition errors (fragments, splits, merges) need an instance-aware signal: put remaining GPU into a model that outputs grouping (N4 detector or a boundary/affinity head), measured by the partition buckets
- **mechanism**: Partition errors are the largest bucket that is not pure annotator disagreement. 148 fragment FPs (+0.014), 58 split FNs (+0.021) and 137 merged FNs (+0.028) total about +0.05 as an oracle, with overlap. Fixed-radius grouping (r=4) cannot choose per pair, and the learned tree selection could not either. A detector predicts instance extent directly, so splits and merges are decided by learned instance shape (the protocol in S14: one piece, includes barbs). This targets 04 (splits) and 05 (merges).
- **evidence**: Measured: buckets above. Group 04: split is 12% of its FNs and near-thin 30% of its FPs. Group 05: merged is 25% of its FNs. Only 14% of fragment-containing GTs are matched by another prediction, so these fragments are not redundant pieces of a TP: their GT is lost entirely. Against: the E8 group channel (a learned grouping signal on the U-Net) gave only +0.003, not distinguishable. External: S9a reports +0.033 for a detector vs their U-Net (whose U-Net was weaker, 0.396); S9c reports voting gains of +0.007 to +0.012.
- **expected_gain_pq**: +0.005 to +0.015 (inference from the bucket ceilings and S9a/S9c; not measured on our data)
- **gpu_hours**: 6
- **dev_hours**: 10
- **cpu_testable_now**: False
- **test_plan**: Control: E9a hyst on the same holdout images, or N1 out-of-fold if it exists. Re-run collect.py/analyze.py on the fused output. Acceptance: ΔPQ ≥ +0.012 with CI > 0 (paired bootstrap, 213 images), AND the fragment + split + merged count falls by at least 25%. Masks must stay non-overlapping (resolve overlaps and assert before submission).
- **risks**: Grouping is itself a major source of annotator disagreement (S14: 'clusters of small nearby filaments skipped'), so a detector could learn one annotator's style and gain nothing, as E8 did. Mask R-CNN masks are coarse (28x28 heads), which can lower SQ on long thin filaments. The cost is the highest of all options against a budget of about 16 GPU h.

### Use the near-miss/extent bucket as the acceptance diagnostic for N3 (blob loss + EMA): too-thin masks on large filaments are the largest fixable model-side loss
- **mechanism**: Near-misses (IoU 0.3-0.5) cost both an FP and an FN. Fixing all 234 gives +0.056; raising every TP to IoU ≥ 0.70 gives +0.033. Large filaments come out too thin: on the 140 near-thin GTs (median 1816 px) the predicted area is a median 0.58 of the GT. Post-processing growth (R4c large_grow) only got +0.005. The tree learner shows extent cannot be chosen from the probability map, so the network must output the annotator-style envelope (barbs included, S14). An instance-level loss (blob loss, S11a) weights each filament equally and penalises under-coverage of large ones, which pixel BCE+Dice does not.
- **evidence**: Measured: near-thin predicted/GT area quartiles 0.47-0.69; group 04 near-thin is 30% of its FPs. 41% of near-miss predictions are not TP for any other annotator, so roughly +0.02 of the +0.056 is plausibly fixable; the rest is extent disagreement between annotators (inference). External: S11a blob-loss bundle +0.009 (90% CI > 0); F13/F17 in RESEARCH.
- **expected_gain_pq**: +0.005 to +0.015
- **gpu_hours**: 3
- **dev_hours**: 3
- **cpu_testable_now**: False
- **test_plan**: Within the pre-registered N3 card: same fold and soft targets as N1 fold 0. Report ΔPQ with CI (pass if ≥ +0.010, CI > 0). Report the near-thin + near-fat FN counts and mean TP IoU from analyze.py: expect near-miss count down at least 20% and SQ up at least 0.01. If PQ rises but near-miss and SQ do not move, the gain is from something else; ablate.
- **risks**: 59% of near-miss predictions on multi-annotator images already satisfy another annotator, so widening masks trades one annotator's IoU against another's. The net could be zero, as with R4c. Blob loss can over-weight tiny components and raise small-filament FPs (the orphan median area is 688 px). Soft multi-annotator targets (N1) may already move extent, so N3 must be measured on top of N1, not on E9a.
